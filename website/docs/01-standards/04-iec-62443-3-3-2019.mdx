## Overview

IEC 62443-3-3 expands the seven **foundational requirements (FRs)** defined in IEC 62443-1-1 into a catalogue of
**system requirements (SRs)**. Each SR has:

- a **baseline requirement** — normative, what the control system shall provide the capability to do;
- **rationale and supplemental guidance** — informative;
- zero or more **requirement enhancements (REs)** — optional add-ons that strengthen the baseline; and
- a **security levels** mapping — which REs (if any) must be layered on top of the baseline to claim SL-C 1 through 4
  for that SR.

:::info What's included in these notes
These notes capture the **seven FRs and their SRs** (baseline requirement + a short rationale).
**Requirement enhancements (REs) are intentionally left out. Security levels (SL-C 1–4) are generally reached by adding one or more REs on top of the baseline requirement.**

The standard's SR catalogue is copyrighted (DS/EN IEC 62443-3-3:2019). To show the format without reproducing the whole catalogue in a public repo, **FR 1 is written out in full as a worked example**, and the later FRs keep the purpose plus a representative SR, with the repetitive remainder marked like this:

> _Remaining SRs omitted to respect the standard's copyright (public repo). The pattern mirrors the SR shown above — see DS/EN IEC 62443-3-3:2019 for the authoritative list._
:::

### Security levels (SL 1–4) — the general pattern

Each FR defines its own SL 1–4 purpose statements, but they all follow the same escalating pattern of adversary
capability:

- **SL 1** — protection against **casual or coincidental** violation.
- **SL 2** — protection against intentional violation using **simple means**, low resources, generic skills, low
  motivation.
- **SL 3** — protection against intentional violation using **sophisticated means**, moderate resources, IACS-specific
  skills, moderate motivation.
- **SL 4** — protection against intentional violation using **sophisticated means**, extended resources, IACS-specific
  skills, high motivation.

Annex A also distinguishes three ways this SL number is used:

- **SL-T** target,
- **SL-A** achieved
- **SL-C** capability — what a component/system can provide (which is what this standard's SRs define).

## Clause 4 — Common control system security constraints

**4.2 Support of essential functions:** security measures shall not adversely affect essential functions.
e.g. accounts used for essential functions shall never be locked out (not even temporarily);
non-repudiation logging shall not add significant response-time delay; Authentication shall not block initiation of a
safety function;

**4.4 Least privilege:** the capability to enforce least privilege shall be provided, with enough
granularity/flexibility in permission-to-role mapping to support it.

## FR 1 — Identification and authentication control (IAC)

**Purpose:** identify and authenticate all users (humans, software processes and devices) before allowing access to
the control system.

- **SR 1.1 — Human user identification and authentication.** Identify and authenticate all human users on every
  interface that provides human access, to support segregation of duties and least privilege.
- **SR 1.2 — Software process and device identification and authentication** _(baseline required from SL 2)_. Same,
  for software processes and devices — protects against rogue entities exchanging data with the control system (e.g.
  man-in-the-middle, spoofing).
- **SR 1.3 — Account management.** Support adding, activating, modifying, disabling and removing accounts, restricted
  to authorized users. Shared/group accounts are acceptable where individual accounts aren't feasible, if compensating
  countermeasures are documented.
- **SR 1.4 — Identifier management.** Support managing identifiers by user, group, role, or control system interface.
- **SR 1.5 — Authenticator management.** Support initializing authenticator content, forcing a change of default
  authenticators at installation, changing/refreshing authenticators, and protecting authenticators (at rest and in
  transit) from disclosure/modification.
- **SR 1.6 — Wireless access management.** Identify and authenticate all users engaged in wireless communication —
  wireless is treated like any other protocol, but physical-security compensating controls are typically weaker, so a
  risk assessment may justify a higher SL-T than the equivalent wired case.
- **SR 1.7 — Strength of password-based authentication.** Where passwords are used, enforce configurable password
  strength (minimum length, character variety).
- **SR 1.8 — Public key infrastructure (PKI) certificates** _(baseline required from SL 2)_. Where PKI is used,
  operate it per accepted best practice, or obtain certificates from an existing PKI.
- **SR 1.9 — Strength of public key authentication** _(baseline required from SL 2)_. Validate certificate signatures,
  build a trust path to an accepted CA (or securely deploy self-signed leaf certificates), check revocation status,
  confirm control of the corresponding private key, and map the authenticated identity to a user.
- **SR 1.10 — Authenticator feedback.** Obscure authentication feedback (e.g. mask password entry) and avoid revealing
  the specific reason for an authentication failure.
- **SR 1.11 — Unsuccessful login attempts.** Enforce a configurable limit on consecutive invalid access attempts
  within a configurable time window, then deny access until unlocked or a timeout elapses; disallow interactive logon
  for accounts running critical services.
- **SR 1.12 — System use notification.** Display a configurable system-use notification/warning banner before
  authentication (mainly a legal/policy control, not a technical IACS-security improvement).
- **SR 1.13 — Access via untrusted networks.** Monitor and control all access methods reaching the control system via
  untrusted networks (dial-up, broadband, wireless, or a connection from the corporate/office network).

## FR 2 — Use control (UC)

**Purpose:** enforce the assigned privileges of an authenticated user (human, software process or device) to perform
only the requested/authorized action, and monitor the use of those privileges.

- **SR 2.1 — Authorization enforcement.** On all interfaces, enforce authorizations assigned to human users, to
  support segregation of duties and least privilege.

> _SR 2.2–2.12 omitted to respect the standard's copyright (public repo). They cover wireless/portable-device use
> control, mobile code, session lock/termination, concurrent-session limits, and the audit chain (auditable events,
> storage capacity, processing-failure response, timestamps, non-repudiation) — the “enforce privileges + record what
> happened” half of FR 2. See DS/EN IEC 62443-3-3:2019 for the authoritative text._

## FR 3 — System integrity (SI)

**Purpose:** ensure the integrity of the IACS to prevent unauthorized manipulation.

- **SR 3.1 — Communication integrity.** Protect the integrity of transmitted information — mitigates in-transit packet
  manipulation (e.g. altering sensor readings or actuator commands).

> _SR 3.2–3.9 omitted to respect the standard's copyright (public repo). They cover malicious-code protection,
> security-functionality verification, software/information integrity at rest, input validation, deterministic
> safe-state output, error handling, session integrity, and protection of audit information — the layered defences for
> when the FR 1/FR 2 controls are bypassed. See DS/EN IEC 62443-3-3:2019 for the authoritative text._

## FR 4 — Data confidentiality (DC)

**Purpose:** ensure the confidentiality of information on communication channels and in data repositories, to prevent
unauthorized disclosure.

- **SR 4.1 — Information confidentiality.** Protect the confidentiality (at rest or in transit) of any information for
  which the control system supports explicit read authorization — the existence of a read-authorization control is
  treated as evidence the information is potentially confidential.

> _SR 4.2–4.3 omitted to respect the standard's copyright (public repo). They cover information persistence (purging
> read-protected data before decommissioning) and use of accepted cryptography (algorithms, key sizes, key lifecycle).
> See DS/EN IEC 62443-3-3:2019 for the authoritative text._

## FR 5 — Restricted data flow (RDF)

**Purpose:** segment the control system via zones and conduits to limit unnecessary data flow.

- **SR 5.1 — Network segmentation.** Logically segment control-system networks from non-control-system networks, and
  critical control-system networks from other control-system networks.
- **SR 5.2 — Zone boundary protection.** Monitor and control communications at zone boundaries to enforce the
  zones-and-conduits model established via the risk assessment (see [IEC 62443-3-2](./03-iec-62443-3-2-2020.mdx)).

> _SR 5.3–5.4 omitted to respect the standard's copyright (public repo). They cover restricting general-purpose
> person-to-person messaging into the control system, and application partitioning by criticality. See DS/EN IEC
> 62443-3-3:2019 for the authoritative text._

## FR 6 — Timely response to events (TRE)

**Purpose:** respond to security violations by notifying the proper authority, reporting evidence, and taking timely
corrective action.

- **SR 6.1 — Audit log accessibility.** Provide authorized humans and/or tools read-only access to audit logs, without
  altering the original records.
- **SR 6.2 — Continuous monitoring** _(baseline required from SL 2)_. Continuously monitor security-mechanism
  performance to detect, characterize, and report security breaches in a timely manner (e.g. IDS/IPS, malicious-code
  protection, network monitoring, often correlated via a security information and event management tool).

## FR 7 — Resource availability (RA)

**Purpose:** ensure the availability of the control system against degradation or denial of essential services.

- **SR 7.1 — Denial of service protection.** Operate in a degraded mode during a DoS event rather than failing
  entirely.

> _SR 7.2–7.8 omitted to respect the standard's copyright (public repo). They cover resource management, control-system
> backup, recovery/reconstitution to a known secure state, emergency power, network/security configuration settings,
> least functionality (restricting unnecessary ports/protocols/services), and component inventory. See DS/EN IEC
> 62443-3-3:2019 for the authoritative text._
