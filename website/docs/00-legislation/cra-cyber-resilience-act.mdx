The Cyber Resilience Act is **Regulation (EU) 2024/2847** of the European Parliament and of the Council.

| Aspect            | Detail                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Instrument**    | Regulation (EU) 2024/2847                                                                                                                   |
| **Scope**         | Cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements across their lifecycle.                                                       |
| **Official text** | [EUR-Lex — Regulation (EU) 2024/2847](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj)                                                       |

## High level breakdown the CRA articles

### Chapter I — General provisions (Art. 1-12)

- **Art. 1** — What the law covers: rules, essential requirements, market surveillance.
- **Art. 2** — Scope: connectable hardware/software; carve-outs for medical devices, vehicles, aviation, defence, spare parts.
- **Art. 3** — Definitions (manufacturer, vulnerability, support period, etc.).
- **Art. 4** — Member states can't block products that comply.
- **Art. 5** — States may add extra requirements when procuring products.
- **Art. 6** — Core rule: products must meet Annex I to be sold.
- **Art. 7** — "Important" product categories (Class I/II) needing stricter checks.
- **Art. 8** — "Critical" product categories that may need mandatory certification.
- **Art. 9** — Commission must consult stakeholders when preparing rules.
- **Art. 10** — Member states should help build cybersecurity skills.
- **Art. 11** — How this law relates to general product safety rules.
- **Art. 12** — Interplay with high-risk AI systems under the AI Act.

### Chapter II — Obligations of operators & open-source (Art. 13-26)

- **Art. 13** — Main manufacturer duties: risk assessment, secure design, docs, CE marking, support period (≥5 years).
- **Art. 14** — Report exploited vulnerabilities & severe incidents (24h/72h/14-day). **Applies from 11 Sep 2026.**
- **Art. 15** — Voluntary reporting of vulnerabilities/incidents by anyone.
- **Art. 16** — ENISA's single reporting platform for notifications.
- **Art. 17** — Other reporting rules (crisis-network sharing, ENISA trend reports, no extra liability for reporting).
- **Art. 18** — What authorised representatives can do on a manufacturer's behalf.
- **Art. 19** — Importer duties: check compliance before importing.
- **Art. 20** — Distributor duties: check CE marking/docs before distributing.
- **Art. 21-22** — When importers, distributors, or modifiers count as manufacturers.
- **Art. 23** — Operators must identify their supply chain on request.
- **Art. 24** — Lighter-touch obligations for open-source software stewards.
- **Art. 25** — Voluntary security attestation for open-source components.
- **Art. 26** — Commission must publish guidance, especially for SMEs.

### Chapter III — Conformity of the product (Art. 27-34)

- **Art. 27** — Presumption of conformity via harmonised standards, common specs, or certification schemes.
- **Art. 28** — What the EU declaration of conformity must state.
- **Art. 29-30** — CE marking rules: how and where to affix it.
- **Art. 31** — What the technical documentation must contain.
- **Art. 32** — Which conformity assessment procedure applies per product class.
- **Art. 33** — Support for SMEs (sandboxes, fee reductions, simplified docs).
- **Art. 34** — Mutual recognition agreements with third countries.

### Chapter IV — Notified bodies (Art. 35-51, applies from 11 Jun 2026)

- **Art. 35-38** — Setting up national notifying authorities.
- **Art. 39-41** — Requirements for conformity assessment bodies (independence, subcontracting).
- **Art. 42-46** — How bodies apply for and get notified; challenging their competence.
- **Art. 47-49** — Notified bodies' operational duties and reporting.
- **Art. 50-51** — Coordination and experience-sharing between notified bodies.

### Chapter V — Market surveillance & enforcement (Art. 52-60)

- **Art. 52** — Market surveillance authorities' roles, cooperation, the Administrative Cooperation Group.
- **Art. 53** — Authorities can request internal docs/data to check compliance.
- **Art. 54** — National procedure for handling risky/non-compliant products.
- **Art. 55** — EU-level safeguard procedure when member states disagree.
- **Art. 56** — Commission-level intervention for significant risks.
- **Art. 57** — Handling compliant products that still pose serious risk.
- **Art. 58** — Formal non-compliance (e.g. missing CE marking or docs).
- **Art. 59** — Joint activities between market surveillance authorities.
- **Art. 60** — Coordinated "sweeps" to check products across the EU.

### Chapter VI — Delegated powers & committee procedure (Art. 61-62)

- **Art. 61** — How the Commission's delegated-act powers work.
- **Art. 62** — Committee procedure for implementing acts.

### Chapter VII — Confidentiality & penalties (Art. 63-65)

- **Art. 63** — Confidentiality obligations for everyone handling CRA data.
- **Art. 64** — Fines: up to €15M / 2.5% turnover for core breaches, lower tiers for others.
- **Art. 65** — Consumers can bring representative (class-action style) claims.

### Chapter VIII — Transitional & final provisions (Art. 66-71)

- **Art. 66-68** — Amendments to related EU laws (market surveillance, representative actions, vehicle type-approval).
- **Art. 69** — Transitional rules: old certificates, grandfathering products already on the market.
- **Art. 70** — Commission must evaluate/review the law periodically.
- **Art. 71** — Entry into force and key application dates.

### Annexes (referenced throughout)

- **Annex I** — The actual essential cybersecurity requirements (product properties + vulnerability handling).
- **Annex II** — Required user information/instructions.
- **Annex III** — "Important" product categories (Class I & II list).
- **Annex IV** — "Critical" product categories.
- **Annex V/VI** — Full and simplified EU declaration of conformity templates.
- **Annex VII** — What must be in the technical documentation.
- **Annex VIII** — The conformity assessment modules (A, B, C, H).
