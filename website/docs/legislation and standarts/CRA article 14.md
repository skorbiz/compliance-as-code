# CRA Article 14

Incident reporting is mostly covered by The Cyber Resilience ACT Article 14.
This section tries to break down _interesting sections_ of Article 14 from the law text.

**Deadline:**

- Article 14 comes into law earlier than the other parts of the CRA.
- Effective Date: 11 September 2026

**Overview:**

Article 14 reporting obligations apply. Manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe security incidents affecting products with digital elements. Deadlines are generally: early warning within 24 hours, full notification within 72 hours, and final reports afterward. This also covers products already placed on the EU market.

### From CRA law: Article 14 Reporting obligations of manufacturers

_Article 14(1) of the Cyber Resilience Act_:
A manufacturer shall notify any **actively exploited vulnerability** to both ENISA and to the country's own platform.

_Article 14(3) of the Cyber Resilience Act_:
A manufacturer shall notify any **severe incident** having an impact on the security to both ENISA and to the country's own platform.

_Article 14(2) and Article 14(4) of the Cyber Resilience Act_:

- Early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware of the issue.
- Within 72 hours: general information, nature of the exploit, mitigating measures taken, mitigating measures users can take, and the sensitivity of the exploit.
- Final report after 14 days.
- Note: Article 14(2) and Article 14(4) say more or less the same thing, but for actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents respectively.

_Article 14(5)_:
Classifies severe incidents as:

- an incident having an impact on the security
- leading to the introduction or execution of malicious code

_Article 14(6) and (7)_:
Authorities can request more information. Article 14(7) specifies which country the reporting should go to if in doubt.

_Article 14(8)_:

- The manufacturer shall inform the impacted users about risk mitigation and corrective measures.
- Where appropriate, in a structured, machine-readable format.
- Where appropriate, the manufacturer shall inform all users (not just impacted ones).
